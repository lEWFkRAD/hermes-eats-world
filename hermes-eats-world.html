Why it exists

The reviewer on a bookkeeping engagement — Chris — had a stroke. His hands and his words are fine. What's gone is procedural recall: he knows what he wants ("print this"), but he can't remember the menu paths and steps to get there anymore.

So the goal was never a clever tool. It was a recognition-over-recall interface: don't make Chris remember the verbs — show them to him. He presses Print; Hermes remembers the menu so he doesn't have to.

The quietly good part: a tool meant to do tasks for someone who can't — was itself built by an agent, running unattended, while no one sat at the desk. "Not bad for 9 hours at a pc" meant the machine's nine hours, not a person's.

Under the hood

Nine modules, two perception tiers, one orchestrator. Tap any card.

🌳perceptionworking
UIA tree walk with COM-hang timeouts (5s/element, 30s total via ThreadPoolExecutor), tier classifier (T1/T2/T3), and a state-delta detector that diffs two snapshots.
🎯targetworking
Find windows by title, process, or class. Drills ApplicationFrameWindow children for UWP apps, with a fallback path.
👆action · T1working
Accessibility-native actions: invoke_element(), set_value(), select_item() via UIA patterns. No pixels — it talks to the controls directly.
📷capture · T2OCR stubbed
Screenshot via mss, DPI-aware NCC template matching, SendInput synthesis for click/type. OCR engine is built but backend='none' — tesseract binary missing, degrades gracefully.
🧭orchestratorworking
Regex intent planner → PERCEIVE→PLAN→ACT→VERIFY executor → retry/fallback/abort recovery. Smoke-tested driving Notepad: "type hello world" in 1.3s, 43 elements seen.
⚙️serviceworking
CLI (--run-goal, --list, --screenshot…), unified perceive_target() API, structured JSON logging, environment/DPI/elevation checks.
🧬schemaworking
Pydantic models + JSON encoder, versioned v1.0.0 from the very first commit — schema evolution without breaking downstream consumers.
✅verifyempty — next
The missing V in the loop. Delta detection lives in perception/delta.py, but the formal pre/post-assertion module is still an empty folder. For Chris this is the "did it actually work, and can he tell?" layer — so it's the next thing that matters.
